1. **High. SKILL.md, Scope vs. "Match the voice": the fabrication rule is dropped for short text.**
   Quoted: "Specificity, plain words, and never fabricating are the universal rules here; the rest yields to context." and, in Scope, "**Text shorter than a paragraph** (a commit subject, a one-line comment, a chat reply): specificity and plain words still apply; skip the rest."
   Problem: the first sentence names three universal rules; the second keeps two and tells the model to skip "the rest", which literally includes "Never invent to sound specific". A one-line chat reply or commit subject is exactly where an invented figure goes unchecked. The same "universal" sentence is also contradicted by the first Scope bullet (a user who asks for buzzwords overrides "plain words") and by the fiction bullet (metaphor overrides "say it literally"). Only "never fabricate" is universal.
   Replacement: in the voice bullet, "Never fabricating is the one rule that does not yield. Specificity and plain words are the defaults; the rest follows the context." In Scope, "**Text shorter than a paragraph**: the rules on claims and plain words apply; the sentence-shape and structure rules have nothing to act on."

2. **High. SKILL.md, "Never invent to sound specific" licenses invented mechanisms, and Example 1 demonstrates it.**
   Quoted (SKILL.md): "With no real example to hand, explain the mechanism or mark the case as hypothetical".
   Quoted (examples/before-after-1.md, After): "Reading a paper map also makes you work out the terrain yourself (contour lines, water, ridgelines), so you are more likely to notice when the trail has left the route you planned." Annotation: "There was no real study to cite, so the rewrite does not swap in another unnamed authority... It explains why the effect would happen and hedges it once".
   Problem: the annotation admits there was no evidence, then the rewrite asserts a causal mechanism as fact. That is a fabricated claim in a less checkable form than "studies show". "Explain the mechanism" reads to a model as permission to confabulate a plausible cause whenever a source is missing. The same After also adds "a phone screen shows a few hundred metres around you" (an invented figure; it depends on zoom) and a closing claim that was not in the original, "For driving across a city, a phone is still the better tool."
   Replacement (SKILL.md): "With no real example to hand, give the mechanism only if you know it, mark a made-up case as hypothetical ('suppose a deploy breaks checkout'), or cut the claim. When rewriting, add no claim the source did not make." Replacement (Example 1): delete the spatial-memory sentence and say in the annotation that "studies show..." was cut because nothing supported it; drop "a few hundred metres" for "only the area around you"; drop or flag the city-driving sentence.

3. **High. SKILL.md checklist item 1 is logically wrong.**
   Quoted: "Is each claim specific and true? If it is neither, cut it."
   Problem: "neither" means not specific and not true. A specific but false claim passes, and so does a true but vague one, although the rule above it wants the vague one replaced. Read loosely the other way (cut anything not both), it deletes true general statements, and in a rewrite it deletes the user's content because the model cannot verify it.
   Replacement: "Is every claim supported by the request or by what you know? Cut or flag any that is not. Can a vague claim be made specific without inventing anything? Do that."

4. **High. SKILL.md, negated-contrast rule: the rule, the checklist, and the table give three different tests, and the strictest one deletes useful corrections.**
   Quoted: "No correcting a view nobody holds ("It's not X, it's Y"): state Y."; checklist 3, "Does any sentence deny something nobody claimed"; table, "Corrects a view no one stated"; examples/before-after-3.md, "Nobody in the passage claimed the cache was slow or proposed a faster hash."
   Problem: "holds", "claimed", "stated", and "in the passage" are different tests. Under "stated in the passage", a correct and useful sentence such as "`git revert` does not delete the commit; it adds a new one that undoes it" is flagged, because the misconception lives in the reader and not in the text. The same goes for an incident report: "The timeout is not in the database. It is in the DNS lookup," when the ticket blamed the database. Example 3's annotation teaches the narrowest test. The table row also files "not only X but also Y" under "Corrects a view no one stated", which is wrong: that construction adds, it does not correct.
   Replacement: use one wording everywhere: "Deny X only when this reader is likely to believe X (a common misconception, the ticket's own diagnosis). Otherwise state Y." Split the table row: "'It's not X, it's Y' | Denies a view the reader does not hold" and "'Not only X but also Y' | Inflates one point into two".

5. **High. SKILL.md, closers: checklist item 2 deletes conclusions, instructions, and requested summaries, and Scope has no exemption.**
   Quoted: "Does the last sentence of each paragraph add a fact? If it only restates, delete it."; table, ""Moreover", "Furthermore", "In conclusion" plus recap".
   Problem: the test is binary (fact or restatement). Final sentences that are neither get caught by the first half: an inference ("So the regression came in with the cache change, not the deploy"), an instruction ("Run the migration before merging"), a request ("Please review by Friday"). Formats whose job is recap (abstract, TL;DR, executive summary, the conclusion section of a long report) are not in Scope, so a model following the skill strips them.
   Replacement: "Does the last sentence of a paragraph only repeat what the paragraph already said? Delete it. A conclusion drawn from the paragraph, an instruction, or a request is not a repeat." Add to Scope: "**Summaries by design** (abstract, TL;DR, the conclusion of a long report): restating is their job."

8. **Medium. SKILL.md, dashes: the rule says "rare", the checklist amounts to "none", and "dash" is undefined.**
   Quoted: "Keep dashes rare; a comma or period usually does the same job." and checklist 6, "Can each dash be a comma, a parenthesis, or a period?"
   Problem: almost any dash can be replaced, so the literal answer to item 6 is always yes and the result is a ban. reference/oss-skills-review.md §5 lists "Bans em dashes outright" as a fault of stop-slop. "Dash" is also unqualified, so a model can apply it to the en dash in a range (SKILL.md itself writes "1.5–2x"), to hyphens, and to `--flag`. The evidence is thin for a rule plus a checklist item plus a table row: 2026-update.md §6 says "The article gives no figures for the size of the em-dash gap", and the corpus is news journalism.
   Replacement: rule, "Put asides in commas or parentheses. Use an em dash only for a break that a comma would hide." Checklist 6: "Is any em dash doing a comma's job? Change it. (Ranges, hyphens, and flags are not em dashes.)"

9. **Medium. SKILL.md, "Say it literally": the model replacement is an empty significance claim, and the rule has no exception for established terms.**
   Quoted: "If a literal phrase is available, use it: "this check matters", not "this check is load-bearing"." Also reference/claude-tics.md: ""earns its keep" for "still matters"".
   Problem: "this check matters" is the unsupported importance claim the first rule and the "significance inflation" row forbid. Example 3's annotation makes the correct move ("each stood in for 'this matters', and the 40% figure already shows that"), but SKILL.md teaches the swap. Separately, a literal phrase is always available at greater length, so the rule as written reaches "bottleneck", "memory leak", "deadlock", "technical debt", "pipeline". The only exception is in claude-tics.md ("the right term for a wall"), which is not loaded.
   Replacement: "**Say it literally.** Replace a figure of speech with the fact it stands for: 'removing this check lets empty orders through', not 'this check is load-bearing'. Established terms that began as metaphors (bottleneck, memory leak) are literal."

15. **Medium. SKILL.md, "Hedge once" will be read as one hedge per document, and "Commit to... claims" pushes against the research the repo cites.**
    Quoted: "**Hedge once, where the doubt is real.**"; "**Commit to specific, checkable claims.**"
    Problem: a report with three uncertain findings needs three hedges. "Once" has no unit, and a model will take the smallest one. 2026-update.md §4 (Röttger et al.) reports that AI assistance already makes writers "seem more opinionated" than they are.     Replacement: "**Hedge each uncertain claim once, at the claim.** Do not stack hedges ('may potentially') and do not hedge what you know." Rename the first rule "**Make claims specific and checkable.**"
